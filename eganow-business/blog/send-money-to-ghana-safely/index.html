How to send money to Ghana safely: a 7-point checklist

Sending money abroad should feel simple and safe. Run through this checklist before you tap “Send”.

Editorial draft. General guidance; needs editorial and compliance approval before publication.
In this article
  1. Check the recipient twice
  2. Look at the rate and the fee together
  3. Choose the right payout method
  4. Know the delivery time
  5. Keep your own account safe
  6. Beware of requests that feel urgent
  7. Keep the receipt

Check the recipient twice

Confirm the recipient’s name, mobile money number or bank account with them directly. A single wrong digit can send money to a stranger, and confirmed transfers may not be reversible.

Look at the rate and the fee together

A low fee can hide a poor exchange rate, and a good rate can hide a high fee. Compare what your recipient will actually receive in cedis, not just the headline rate. With Eganow, the rate and fee are shown before you confirm.

Choose the right payout method

Mobile money wallets (MTN, Telecel, AirtelTigo) are usually fastest and suit everyday needs. Bank deposits suit larger amounts or recipients without a wallet. Ask your recipient which they prefer.

Know the delivery time

Transfers are often completed within minutes, but cut-off times, bank hours and verification checks can add time. Plan important payments (school fees, rent) with a buffer.

Keep your own account safe

Never share your PIN, password or one-time codes, including with someone claiming to be from your provider. Eganow will never ask for them.

Beware of requests that feel urgent

Scammers pretend to be relatives or officials in an emergency. Call the person on a number you already know before sending money.

Keep the receipt

Save the confirmation and reference number. If something goes wrong, they help support find your transfer quickly.
