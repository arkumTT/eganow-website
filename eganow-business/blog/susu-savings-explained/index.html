Susu savings, explained: how group saving works

Susu is one of the oldest ways to save together. Here is how it works and how to run one without the paperwork headaches.

Editorial draft. General guidance; needs editorial and compliance approval before publication.
In this article
  1. What is susu?
  2. Why people love it
  3. Where susu goes wrong
  4. Make it transparent
  5. Start small and review

What is susu?

In a susu, a group of people agree to contribute a fixed amount on a regular schedule. Each round, one member receives the pooled money, until everyone has had a turn.

Why people love it

It builds a saving habit, gives members access to a lump sum they might struggle to save alone, and relies on community trust.

Where susu goes wrong

Missed contributions, unclear rules and poor records cause most disputes. A trusted organiser and a clear record help.

Make it transparent

Agree the amount, schedule, payout order and what happens if someone is late. Write it down and share it with every member. A tool that logs every contribution automatically (like Groups in the Eganow app) shows who has paid and when.

Start small and review

Begin with a small group of people you know well, run a few cycles, then adjust the rules based on what you learn.
