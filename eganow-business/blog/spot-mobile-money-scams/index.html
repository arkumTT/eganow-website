Seven signs of a mobile money scam (and what to do)

Fraudsters change their stories, but their tricks repeat. Learn the signs and you can stop most scams in seconds.

Editorial draft. General guidance; needs editorial and compliance approval before publication.

  2. 2. “Send money to unlock your account”
  3. 3. You must act right now

1. Someone asks for your PIN or a one-time code

No genuine provider will ever ask for these, by call, text, chat or in person. End the contact.

2. “Send money to unlock your account”

Real providers do not charge you to release your own money or to “verify” an account. Paying a fee to receive money is a classic scam.

3. You must act right now

Pressure and countdown threats are designed to stop you thinking. Slow down and check through official channels.

4. A friend or relative needs money in a hurry

Hijacked accounts and cloned numbers are common. Call the person on a number you know before you pay.

5. Prizes, refunds or “wrong transfers”

Messages claiming you won, or that money was sent to you by mistake and must be returned, are usually attempts to take your money.

6. Links and fake agent points

Only download apps from the App Store or Google Play. At an agent point, check you are at a genuine, identified agent before handing over cash or details.

7. Requests to install software or share your screen

This gives criminals control of your phone. Never agree.

If you have been caught out

Contact your provider straight away, freeze your cards in the app, change your PIN and keep the messages as evidence. Then report it so others are protected.
